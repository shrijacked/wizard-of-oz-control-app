## Abstract: every sentence accounted for

### A1. Conceptual motivation

In human--robot interaction (HRI), deciding when to provide assistance requires understanding how different intervention strategies affect both task performance and the user experience.

**Evidence:** Introduction and the bounded assistance-timing/appropriateness sources [1], [10], [21], [31]. This is motivation, not a measured finding.

### A2. Literature precedent

Previous work has compared physiology-triggered robotic assistance with periodic support in surgical training.

**Evidence:** Yang [33], PDF pp8-10: adaptive-versus-periodic robotic suction in surgical training simulation.

### A3. Study design

We extend this work to physical spatial problem solving through a within-participant experiment comparing three conditions: no assistance, constant assistance every 30 seconds, and adaptive assistance triggered by a short-term heart-rate rise.

**Evidence:** Sections 4.2-4.4: control, constant 30-second help, HR-rise triggering; physical tangram task.

### A4. Sample and attempts

Twenty-four participants completed nine physical tangram attempts across three blocks.

**Evidence:** Section 4.4 and aggregate analysis: 24 participants, nine attempts each across three blocks, 216 attempts in total. Completed attempts does not mean all puzzles were solved.

### A5. Condition order and puzzle allocation

Condition orders were balanced across participants, and puzzles were randomly assigned to blocks.

**Evidence:** Section 4.4 and supplementary allocation table: four participants per condition order; puzzles randomly assigned to blocks, with uneven realized puzzle allocation.

### A6. Task rationale

Tangrams combine spatial reasoning with physical manipulation, providing a simple task for studying informational and physical assistance.

**Evidence:** Sections 3 and 4.1: physical tangrams require spatial reasoning and manipulation; hints convey placement information and the arm retrieves physical pieces.

### A7. Delivery

Participants received on-screen hints and robotic-arm interventions through a Wizard-of-Oz setup, with the assigned condition determining intervention timing and task progress guiding assistance content.

**Evidence:** Section 4.1 and apparatus: restricted preset hints and pickup-availability-controlled robot retrieval.

### A8. Measures

We evaluated puzzle completion, correct-piece progress, recorded round duration, workload, helpfulness, intervention timing, frustration, and study-wide trust.

**Evidence:** Section 4.5: completion, correct pieces, descriptive recorded round duration, seven-point adapted NASA-TLX, frustration, assistance items and study-wide trust. Hart [11] supports the TLX dimensions, not our adaptation.

### A9. Progress, completion, workload and frustration

Both assistance conditions improved completion and correct-piece progress and reduced workload compared with no assistance (Holm-adjusted $p < .001$); both also reduced frustration.

**Evidence:** Twelve-test main paired family: all six assisted-control comparisons for success, pieces and TLX have Holm p<.001. Frustration: constant-control Holm p=.0268948797 and adaptive-control p<.001.

### A10. Descriptive objective/subjective pattern

Descriptively, constant assistance achieved higher completion and correct-piece progress, while adaptive assistance received higher helpfulness and timing ratings and lower workload and frustration.

**Evidence:** Tables 1-3: constant exceeds adaptive in completion (59.7% vs 50.0%) and pieces (5.68 vs 5.43); adaptive has lower workload (46.06 vs 49.50) and frustration (3.51 vs 3.93), and higher means on all four assistance items. This is descriptive, not significant superiority; effort is an exception among workload dimensions.

### A11. Conclusion

Together, these findings demonstrate benefits of assistance in physical spatial problem solving and suggest potential trade-offs between task performance and user experience that warrant further investigation.

**Evidence:** Synthesis bounded by the main/adjusted contrasts and package/dose/physiological-validity limitations in Section 6.
